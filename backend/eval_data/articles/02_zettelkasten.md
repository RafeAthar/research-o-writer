# Sample Article: The Zettelkasten Method

> **Note:** Synthetic test fixture for the Research-o-Writer eval harness.
> Not a real publication.

## 1. Origin

The Zettelkasten ("slip-box") method is a personal knowledge-management
practice associated with the German sociologist Niklas Luhmann. Luhmann
maintained a paper-based slip-box of approximately 90,000 cards over several
decades and credited it as a co-author of his published work.

## 2. Atomicity

The defining principle is atomicity: each card holds exactly one idea,
expressed in the author's own words. Quotation is discouraged. The discipline
of restating the idea in one's own language is part of how the method
encodes understanding rather than merely accumulating notes.

## 3. Linking

Atomic cards become useful only when they are densely linked. New cards are
filed next to the most closely related existing card and given an explicit
reference identifier so that they can be cited from other cards. The
emergent structure is closer to a directed graph than to a hierarchy.

## 4. Index and entry points

A small index of high-level topics serves as the entry-point set. The index
is intentionally sparse: it lists a handful of starting cards per topic
rather than every card on that topic. Discovery proceeds from the entry
point outward through links.

## 5. What the method is not

The Zettelkasten is not a search engine, a summary system, or a daily
journal. Confusing it with any of these tends to produce a slip-box that
quickly becomes useless. It is a thinking tool whose value lies in the act
of writing and linking cards, not in retrieval after the fact.
